# Anarchy Online: The Animated Series (2001)

> The working log as it was kept during the restoration, 26–30 Oct 2026, lightly edited for publication (local paths, long press quotes).
> Claude wrote it as the work went; "you" and "your call" mean the person reviewing and deciding. It refers to
> project folders (`originals/`, `upscaled/`, `review/`…) that hold video and aren't in this repository.

Funcom's animated series for Anarchy Online: four episodes released online from 31 October to 18 December 2001,
told alongside the game's live story. Episode 104 came out with patch 13.4, which put the bombed apartment block from
the episode into Omni-1 Entertainment. Funcom ended the series after 104 (announced late December 2001); there is no
episode 105.

**Release dates from the forum archive (30 Oct 2026).** The [Anarchaeology forum archive](https://anarchaeology.github.io/forums/)
has the 2001 launch forum from the May 2001 beta to its last saved post on 27 Sep 2001 (it was frozen that month; it
never mentions the series, and in September players are still waiting for "the beginning of the story line"), then
Funcom's main forum from the day it opened, 15 Nov 2001 (its thread #1 at 15:34 GMT). Nothing survives from between,
so nothing on 101's release. Times are GMT, as the forum showed them. Every two weeks, on Wednesdays:
- **101:** Wed 31 Oct 2001 (Halloween, which the episode is about). GameSpot, "Anarchy Online story begins", posted
  31 Oct 2001 at 15:55 PST (23:55 GMT): the storyline "begins today", with the first episode on the official story site; Funcom hoped to bring
  "central players from the game" into the episodes (which Snarf on Radiman's desk in 104 then did). GameSpot's "storyline impressions"
  that week opens "On Wednesday, October 31, Funcom began the storyline arc". Funcom's FTP
  folder `/storyline/`, as the Wayback saved it on 2 Nov 2001 02:39 UTC, held only the intro preview (uploaded 30 Oct
  17:18); the folder above it changed on 31 Oct 11:39. Players watched 101 as Windows Media files from the story site;
  the MPEG parts went onto the FTP later. In the forum, players later speak of "the halloween episode", and a player
  Ragnar answered in July 2002 recalls "we were told October 31 and everyone logged on then".
- **102:** on 15 Nov, within two hours of the new forum opening, people have the new episode and ask for its playlist; "There isn't an asx
  for the new episode on the ftp server" ([thread 89](https://anarchaeology.github.io/forums/thread/89.html)). Two weeks
  after 31 Oct is Wed 14 Nov.
- **103:** "Chess Game" starts at 17:37 on Wed 28 Nov, a nitpick about the moves called in 103's chess game
  ([thread 2247](https://anarchaeology.github.io/forums/thread/2247.html)).
- **104:** Funcom moved it from Wed 12 Dec to Tue 18 Dec "because it contains links with the 13.4 patch"
  ([thread 3897](https://anarchaeology.github.io/forums/thread/3897.html)). The patch was due live on the morning of the
  19th; the forum's first "Go watch Episode 4, it has been made available on the ftp-site!" is 19 Dec 12:00 GMT, the same day
  the bombed apartment block appeared in Omni-1 Entertainment.

**From the forum (30 Oct 2026):** Ragnar Tørnquist on why the series stopped (20-26 Jul 2002, ["I miss the animated
series!"](https://anarchaeology.github.io/forums/thread/39274.html)): episodes of 10-15 minutes every two weeks were
"waaay beyond our limited means"; "The animation-team almost killed themselves getting the first four episodes out on
time. I'm talking 16-18 hour days, plus weekends." It was made by the Midgard team after that project was put on hold,
so it took nothing from the AO live team; pre-rendered because the engine couldn't do cinematics or lip-sync. Also:
Snarf, a real player (Atrox NT, leader of The Shadows of Rubi-Ka, self-styled Mayor of Tir), is the 'SNARF' profile on
Radiman's desk in 104 (104 at 4:08, the series video at 41:25), spotted the day it came out
([thread 4592](https://anarchaeology.github.io/forums/thread/4592.html)) and rediscovered into late 2002.

**Is anything missing? (checked 30 Oct 2026)** No, as far as every source shows.

  [39274](https://anarchaeology.github.io/forums/thread/39274.html) and [41009](https://anarchaeology.github.io/forums/thread/41009.html).

## Restoration plan (decided after testing, 26–27 Oct 2026)

Tested on four 10-second clips from Episode 104 (a face close-up, a dark bar interior, the chess scene, the opening
titles) and 70 seconds of Episode 101. The test files were cleared afterwards; these are the conclusions.

- **Upscaler: Topaz Starlight Precise 2.6, sharpness 1 (the lowest), 4×, cloud render** (the RTX 2070 Super, 8 GB,
  can't run any Starlight model locally). It looked good on faces, darkness, detail and titles. Sharpness 3 was
  crunchier. Starlight Mini looked worse than Proteus. Precise invents plausible surface detail (e.g. weathering on a
  vehicle's hull), which was judged acceptable.
- **Where Starlight misreads something, patch just that spot with Proteus 4 (auto) at 4×** through a soft mask that
  follows the object. Example: in Episode 101 (about 1:07–1:13 of the episode) the distant hover-truck became a rock
  with two "figures" (really its antennas); Proteus shows the real truck, crisp, and blends in. The more ambitious fix
  was tried and rejected: Topaz Astra 2 (web app, creative mode with a text prompt) drew a ship, but not the one the
  shot pans to.
- **Preparing inputs:** crop the black bars (352×188 at y=26), keep the pixel shape tag (200:219), constant 29.97 fps,
  ProRes 422 HQ with standard SD colour tags (BT.601). Topaz rejected lossless H.264 (High 4:4:4) without colour tags;
  its web app rejects ProRes but takes high-quality H.264.
- **Topaz settings:** frame interpolation, stabilization, grain, focus fix, motion deblur, denoise and SDR-to-HDR all
  off. For patches, Proteus at 4× with square pixels gives ~1288×752, matching Starlight's 1284×752.
- **Starlight cloud jobs are limited to about 5 minutes (9,000 frames):** `aoe101c` (5:31) has to be split, ideally at a
  scene change. Cost quoted at about 5 credits per 10 seconds, so roughly 1,550 credits for the whole series.
- Topaz writes each export's settings into the file (`videoai` / `topazcloud` tags: `prob-4` = Proteus 4,
  `slm-1` = Starlight Mini, `slp-2.6` = Starlight Precise 2.6; the sharpness level isn't recorded), so outputs can be
  identified even when the queue doesn't say.
- **After rendering:** flag shots where Starlight changed what's there (shrink back to 352×188 and compare with the
  original, shot by shot), review that list by eye, patch, then join in Funcom's order with chapters and the original
  audio, scale to the final size (1080p, or 4K for YouTube's better encoding) and encode one MP4.

## Upscaled (27 Oct 2026)

All 18 upload files came back from Topaz Cloud as `upload/NN …_starlight.mov`: Starlight Precise 2.6 (`slp-2.6`),
1284×752, ProRes 422 HQ, every frame present (counts match the inputs), converted to BT.709 colour and tagged so. Sharpness

Review stage:

- A frame-by-frame comparison (shrink the upscale back to the original's size, find the block that differs most) points
  at the most-changed spot in each frame, but its scores don't single out misreads: busy action shots score higher than

- `review/fix_list.md`: spots to fix or judge, from Claude looking over 1,014 sampled frames with close-ups (it found

Proteus (for patches and whole-shot swaps), decided 27 Oct 2026:

- Rendered locally by `proteus_render.js` (`tools/`) through Topaz's own ffmpeg
  (`C:\Program Files\Topaz Labs LLC\Topaz Video\ffmpeg.exe`, the same `tvai_up` filter the app runs; a test on the
  truck frames came out bit-identical to the app's export). About 11–12 frames a second on the RTX 2070 Super.
- Settings: Proteus 4 (`prob-4`), auto mode, recover original detail 20 (`blend=0.2`), everything else 0, no grain.
  Chosen over 50 from side-by-sides of four shots (`review/proteus_tests/`). Proteus smooths away the masked man's
  grain at both settings.
- Format made to drop in beside Starlight: squeezed to 321×188 then 4× = 1284×752 square pixels (the app's default

  Checked against Starlight: same frame count, matching colour, best alignment at zero shift.
- `upload/NN …_proteus.mov`: all 18 parts (33.3 GB, rendered in about 40 minutes, ~39 frames a second). Settings and
  source range are in each file's tags. Checked: every part has the same frame count as its input and its Starlight

- Rendering whole parts gives the same result as rendering a shot alone (the four test shots match at 64–98 dB),
  except the first ~6 frames after a hard cut, which carry a faint trace of the previous shot (about 40 dB, not

### Claude's remaining Episode 101 notes (27 Oct)

The two of Claude's notes you wanted fixed, plus one decided from the episode scripts. Review clips are in
`review/fix shots/`.

- **U9, nebula streak** (`01 aoe101a`, frames 990-1100). This is a faint diagonal streak in Funcom's artwork that
  Starlight (and Proteus) draw as a sharp line as the camera flies in. `tools/line_remove.js` follows it from rough
  keyframes (`tools/data/nebula_line/keys.tsv`), finds it exactly in each frame, and fills a band along it from the
  nebula either side. Before, the streak sat 7-13 luma levels below its surroundings; after, 1-3, about the
  nebula's own texture. The vertical streak and its faint horizontal partner are a starburst (a lens-flare-like
  cross) and stay, as you asked. Approved 27 Oct.
- **U10, sky through her hair** (`02 aoe101b`, frames 5825-5880, behind Eleena's head as the pumpkin man appears).
  Arching strands near the top and sides of her hair enclose small pockets of sky, in the original too.
  `tools/hair_fill.js --only=sky` fills enclosed sky-coloured pockets with the hair's colour, and fades out at the
  focus pull (see below). The skin-coloured gaps at the nape are left alone (your call). After your "still some
  small gaps", a second pass also takes the dark-blue specks and rims (sky seen through thin strands, dark enough to
  pass for hair by brightness alone, but blue where the hair is dark red) and the tiniest specks. Then the sky
  flashed through around 5870, as the focus pulls: the blurring pockets stop looking enclosed, so the fill stopped
  while the soft blue spots were still visible. Now the last sharp frame's gaps (5865) are carried through
  5866-5877 (`--hold`). They follow her head, soften with the picture (up to 5 px), and take their colour from the
  blurred picture around each spot, so they don't show as dark blobs. Then the fill fades out, once the spots have
  blurred away. Approved 27 Oct.
- **Eva Pourais** (`03 aoe101c-1`, frames 4196-4445, the woman seated at the back of Ross's office). The 101 script
  names her: she has listened in on the Radiman call. Funcom left her out of focus. Starlight renders her sharp, with
  grey hair pulled back and an older face. That contradicts Episode 104, where she has close-ups (`17 aoe104c` at
  about 0:12-0:57, `18 aoe104d` at about 0:39): jet-black straight bob, pale sharp face. So she gets an oval fix that
  keeps her soft. You compared Proteus (detail 20) with Starlight blurred 1.5 + grain, as for Ross
  (`review/fix shots/U11 Eva - pick A or B.mp4`), and picked blurred + grain (U11). That leaves her hair dark around a
  pale face, close to 104. Laying the original over just her head, to darken the hair further, was tried and dropped:
  it left a faint doubled outline.

### Joining an episode without loss (27 Oct, tested on 101)

`tools/make_episode.js` joins an episode's fixed parts into one master: `final/Episode 101 - Tricks and Treats
(master).mov` (15.6 GB, 16:38). How it's built:

- **Picture:** copied frame for frame, with no re-encode (ProRes 422 HQ, 1284×752).
- **Sound:** the original PCM sound. Each part's sound is trimmed to exactly its picture's length, because the original
  MPEGs' sound runs 2-25 ms long and would otherwise drift about 80 ms out of sync by the end.
- **Chapters:** Teaser, Act 1, Act 2, Act 3, Tag. They're also in `final/Episode 101 - chapters.txt`, in YouTube's
  description format.
- **Colour tags:** BT.709 (header only).

Checks:

- All 29,926 frames decode identically to the fixed parts (MD5 per frame).
- The sound is sample for sample the trimmed originals.
- Picture and sound end within 0.03 ms of each other.
- At the 101c rejoin the sound matches Funcom's unsplit `aoe101c.mpg` at zero offset (correlation 1.00000).
- The movie timescale is set to the frame clock (30000), because ffmpeg's default chapter track overflowed QuickTime's
  32-bit durations on a full episode.

Disk: each episode master is about 15 GB, and 63 GB was free after 101's.

the DivX and `_low.wmv` versions weren't found anywhere and would be no better.
